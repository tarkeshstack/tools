Domestic Travel

Getting Around India

The practical half of the trip, sorted once so the rest of it can be about the places above.

Trains
Book through IRCTC. General quota opens up to 120 days ahead; Tatkal opens the day before travel (10am for AC classes, 11am for non-AC). Rajdhani, Shatabdi and Vande Bharat trains are the fastest options on busy routes.
Flights
IndiGo, Air India, Vistara and SpiceJet cover most of the domestic network. Fares climb fast closer to the date — book 4–6 weeks out where the dates allow it.
Cabs & local transport
Ola, Uber and Rapido (bike taxis) work in most cities. Smaller towns, hill stations and islands rely on local taxi unions and pre-agreed fares rather than app cabs.
Permits
Inner Line Permits are required for parts of Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh and the Sikkim border; some Andaman zones need a tribal reserve permit. Check the specific route ahead — rules change by stretch and season.
Money & payments
UPI is accepted almost everywhere in cities and larger towns. Carry some cash for rural areas, autos and small vendors — ATMs thin out in remote hill and island regions.
Packing by season
Light cottons for the plains, October–March. Warm layers for hill stations and the Himalayas year-round. Monsoon gear if you're travelling June–September, especially in the hills.

On the ground

  • Shoes off before entering temples, gurdwaras and most homes — look for the shoe rack at the door.
  • Dress modestly at religious sites; a scarf to cover shoulders or head is worth keeping in your bag.
  • Ask before photographing people, especially at ghats and in villages — a nod and a smile go a long way.
  • Right hand for eating, giving and receiving — the left is considered unclean by long-standing convention.
  • Agree the fare first with unmetered autos and rickshaws, and with boatmen at ghats and backwaters.
  • Keep copies of your ID separate from the originals, and use registered taxis after dark in unfamiliar towns.
